About

Modelling small-scale dynamics through stochasticity

I have defended my PhD thesis at INRIA and IRMAR (University of Rennes, 2026), which was supervised by Arnaud Debussche (ENS Rennes / IRMAR) and Étienne Mémin (INRIA / IRMAR). So far, my work sits at the intersection of stochastic analysis and fluid mechanics, built on the location uncertainty (LU) formalism developed by Mémin and collaborators.

Very loosely, LU introduces a small-scale, rigorously constructed noise term, to model the effects of unresolved dynamics on the large-scale one. It lets you derive an entire family of models — geophysical flows, reduced-order models, large-eddy simulations — from the same conservation principles used in the deterministic setting.

I am as interested in the physical background behind the stochasticity as I am in proving theorems the arising models. I would like to keep working on stochastic PDEs connected to physics, and am open to discovering fields of application beyond fluid mechanics.

Well-posedness

A stochastic ocean model: the primitive equations

With my supervisors, I built a stochastic interpretation of the oceanic primitive equations that relaxes the classical hydrostatic assumption, keeping stochastic terms in the vertical momentum balance as mechanical deviations from equilibrium. This moves the model closer to the true three-dimensional problem, at the cost of a far more complex pressure structure — which we regularise with a low-pass filter. We proved global-in-time well-posedness for the regularised system, in the spirit of the Debussche-Glatt-Holtz–Ziane and Brzezniak-Slavik arguments. We also used many insights provided by the stochastic-maximal-regularity setting of Agresti-Veraar — see Agresti et al. (2021) — and use Cao–Titi's barotropic/baroclinic splitting for the key estimates.

Asymptotics

From Navier–Stokes to the primitive equations

I studied the vanishing-aspect-ratio limit of the LU Navier–Stokes equations toward the LU primitive equations, adapting Li & Titi's (2019) deterministic proof scheme to the stochastic setting. Itô's formula forces the introduction of a modified Leray-type projector to handle the pressure terms; under periodic boundary conditions the convergence can be upgraded from a weak L²–H¹ norm to a strong H¹–H² one. The result exposes a "grey zone" — governed by the aspect ratio and noise-intensity parameters — where the relaxed-hydrostatic model approximates 3D Navier–Stokes better than the classical hydrostatic one. Submitted to Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis.

Modelling · Numerics

Stochastic wave models

Applying the LU formalism to conservation of mass and momentum yields stochastic counterparts of the Saint-Venant, Boussinesq, and Serre–Green–Naghdi equations for coastal waves — which I implemented in 1D and 2D with direct and pseudo-spectral methods. More recently I have been deriving an LU interpretation of the full Water Waves equations, building on Dinvay–Mémin (2022) and Lannes (2013). In the small-aspect-ratio limit, it formally recovers stochastic interpretations of these shallow-water models, and it opens a route toward understanding extreme waves as extreme realisations of stochastic dynamics.

An example of wave model

The LU Saint-Venant equations

These equations read,

\[ \begin{aligned} d_t\eta &+ \big[(u\,dt + \sigma_H dW_t)\circ\nabla_H\big]\eta = -(h_0+\eta)\,\nabla_H\!\circ\!\big(u\,dt + \sigma_H dW_t\big),\\[4pt] d_t u &+ \big[(u\,dt + \sigma_H dW_t)\circ\nabla_H\big]u + \nabla_H \eta\, dt = 0, \end{aligned} \]

where η is the water height, u the depth-averaged horizontal velocity, and σHdWt the noise-driven horizontal displacement. In addition, \(\circ\) stands for the Stratonovitch product. Set the noise to zero and you recover the classical deterministic Saint-Venant system: the location uncertainty formalism only adds a perturbation on top of familiar physics.
